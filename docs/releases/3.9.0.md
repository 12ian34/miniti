### Integrated account transition

One review explains the destination account, which eligible meetings will be copied or left local, and that Pro, consumed usage and connections stay with their source account. Do not introduce account merging or enable cloud, recording automation or CRM sending through sign-in. Preserve B4's explicit treatment of unknown legacy meetings and locally readable meetings left behind.

This review is available only under the active ownership capability defined below. The [B3 entry-point matrix](../plans/account-ownership.md#account-change-entry-points) covers restore/new enrollment, reused-ID create after revoke, genuine new-ID create, recovery-key attach, email attach/restore, Migration Assistant recovery, sign-out, deletion, remote removal, Cloud on/off and Delete cloud copy, with Linux equivalents. Do not implement only the ordinary attach button.

Before committing a transfer, settle or retain owner-bound pending work under AO0's finalized authorization contract, establish B1/B2/B5 source ownership and obtain B4 copy authority while still authorized on the source. Bind the copy grant to the destination before the membership commit, then apply B3's membership transition, invalidate old-account cached/queued authority and reconcile B5 routing. Resume copies idempotently under the destination checks. The final implementation must define/rehearse failures at each boundary across Redis/Postgres; do not treat this sequence as an atomic multi-store transaction.

Test sign-out, switch, remote device removal and account deletion separately. Cloud/API/MCP access must use durable account entitlement and the applicable authorization policy; removing the last device must not accidentally erase the account's paid entitlement. Deletion/revocation still invalidate the credentials/tokens they own. Account deletion's existing separate upstream-billing cancellation behavior remains explicit in the review.

## Confirmed compatibility and entitlement decisions

### Old-client transition gate, AO4

The shipped Apple recovery-key switch screen says “This device keeps its meetings, usage, calendar and CRM connections” (`ManagedAccountViews.swift:141`); the email-switch screen says “This device keeps its meetings, usage and connections and joins that account” (`:2137`). The different repair/summary strings at `:318` and `:1612` preserve local meetings and destination-eligible Pro; the audit registers each exact string separately. Line 141 also promises immediate closure of an emptied source, which B3 replaces with dormancy. Linux has the connection promise at `Settings.tsx:800` and the success text “Its meetings, usage and connections came with it” at line 730. Existing binaries cannot be corrected remotely. Consent to those screens does not authorize silently losing a buying device's Pro or stopping its calendar auto-start/automatic CRM sends. Usage/meeting changes are not the harmful compatibility cases identified in this review, although they still require migration tests.

At server stage S2, before any B1/B2/B5 ownership semantics become effective, gate `POST /api/auth/account/attach`, the email-join paths, restore-into-account and `/api/auth/account/create` reusing an installation previously bound to another account by platform/version. Choose stable HTTP **426 `update_required`**. Mac/iOS minimum compatible versions are **3.9.0** under the currently selected scope; Linux must supply its own first compatible version before S2. Unknown platforms, missing/malformed versions and below-threshold clients refuse. This is blanket by version for these operations, never conditional on having a subscription or connection. If the split is confirmed, revise these thresholds before S2 so a UI-only 3.9.0 is never treated as ownership-compatible. Compatible releases must already be publicly obtainable on all three platforms before S2; the gate cannot direct anyone to an unavailable update.

Check before consuming a single-use link, revoking/replacing credentials, binding a grant, adopting tokens or changing membership. Preserve the gate on retries and all post-S2 rollback targets. Ordinary recording, usage reads, sign-out, removal and deletion are not blanket force-updated by this route gate. Their cleanup must preserve source ownership evidence before re-enrollment can replace the installation record.

**Recovery exception, operation-based:** after existing recovery-key or approved-email authorization, a server-proven same-account restore/rekey (`existing.accountId === target`, including a revoked installation) and an exact authenticated lost-response replay may proceed on old or new clients during `preparing`/`paused`. Retain account-active/deletion checks, device-cap rules where membership is re-added, credential-generation invalidation and authoritative source-asset checks. A device ID alone or a client claim of repair is insufficient. It must neither change account ownership nor adopt a foreign/unresolved asset. This exception preserves Migration Assistant and sign-in recovery; it is not a subscription/connection-dependent exception to the transfer gate. Brand-new-device restore into an existing account remains gated and waits for S5, even if it has no device assets; no asset-empty exemption is approved. Genuine fresh account creation on a new/rotated ID stays available without adopting old-ID assets.

**Email preflight:** compatible clients check capability/version before offering a cross-account email join, and pending links are rechecked at commit. Do not blanket-refuse the unauthenticated `/api/auth/email/start` endpoint for old clients: it also starts same-account recovery, and the target cannot safely be disclosed or classified before email proof. Preserve uniform known/unknown-email responses. An early server refusal is allowed only where the operation can be classified without weakening repair or leaking account existence; the mandatory final gate stays before link consumption. Test old same-account email repair, unknown emails, old cross-account refusal after approval and updated-client retry of that unconsumed link.

Old Apple clients show **“Server error (426): update_required”** through `ClientAuth.swift:644`. Ian accepts that rare-flow fallback; the new client provides a clear Update action using the existing platform updater/store. Verify the actual older Apple and Linux presentations and their update path, without claiming that the generic old error itself contains a new button. Tests: old Mac, old iOS, old Linux, new clients, exact version boundary, missing/invalid versions, empty accounts and accounts with no subscriptions/connections, email-link retry after update and zero mutation on refusal.

### Server-authoritative StoreKit behavior, AO2

The backend alone decides Pro and quota eligibility. Local `Transaction.updates`/`currentEntitlements` trigger `/api/apple/verify`; they never OR into `isPro`, bypass `isLimitReached` or override a server refusal. Immediately after a purchase on this device, show a bounded “confirming your subscription” pending state, then server-confirmed Pro or an actionable retry. A failed verification never leaves optimistic Pro or an indefinite pending state.

On `subscription_owned_elsewhere`, stay free on this miniti account, with recording available within its free allowance. End the “Pro is active, but usage sync is still catching up” loop. Do not show a launch error. Show a calm Account card and, for fresh installs/guests, a setup card: **“Your App Store subscription is on another miniti account. Sign in to that account to use Pro here.”** Actions: sign in with email, sign in with recovery key, manage subscription. Apple ID ownership never switches miniti accounts automatically; shared family Apple IDs must not transfer account access.

Recommend evaluating StoreKit `appAccountToken` on new purchases to bind the purchase to the initiating miniti account, analogous to Polar checkout attribution. Implementation must verify the supported token format and choose a stable account-to-token mapping (miniti's `acct_…` ID is not directly a UUID), receipt/server verification and old-transaction handling. This remains an implementation decision, not a promise that all old receipts contain attribution.

Record an admin counter of `subscription_owned_elsewhere` verify outcomes by platform/app version, with no customer data. Old iOS 3.8.0 can retain the misleading loop even with the transition gate, especially after reinstall into a guest account. At S3/S5 rollout review, inspect event counts and affected-installation aggregates over a defined observation window. Ian must explicitly decide whether the count is non-trivial enough to raise the iOS minimum version; define/record that threshold before observation, never auto-bump it. Include normal verify traffic as the denominator. The route gate is unconditional; this broader force-update decision is conditional.

Establish a pre-S2 baseline and separate S2–S5 guest creation, refused restores and subsequent owned-elsewhere outcomes in bounded aggregate diagnostics. A rollout-induced rise is a migration incident to investigate, not evidence that unrelated customers inherently need a force update. Never automatically create a guest after a refused sign-in; preserve the recovery choice and show temporary unavailability on compatible clients.

Required tests: fresh reinstall/guest with an existing App Store subscription, family Apple ID on another miniti account, purchase and verify on the current account, owned-elsewhere card followed by email/key sign-in restoring Pro, free recording while the card is visible, held transaction updates after account change, no spontaneous launch alert and no retry/message loop.
