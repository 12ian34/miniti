## Field investigation, 2026-11-14

Ian reported "ServiceError" in Settings, Cloud and a run of operator alert emails, asked for a full investigation and asked for it to be planned into 3.10.0. Unlike the 2026-11-11 pass, this one made read-only production reads, at Ian's request:

- Admin ops health, client incidents and client events through `scripts/admin-stats.mjs`. Cached reads only, no `--refresh`, so no alert email was triggered.
- Vercel production request logs: method, path, status, device prefix and app version.
- Ian's own account in Neon: change history, document keys, folder fields, sizes and device columns. No transcript text was read.
- KV: Ian's account record; one scan of the cloud switch fields on all 266 account records, counts only; installation counts for the 3 accounts that have the switch stored.
- A scratch git worktree of the backend at `196fe8d`, to run the unit suite with a KV stub that deserializes hash reads the way production does. It was removed afterwards.

No write, deploy, provider call, alert refresh or customer contact was made, and the investigation changed no file in either working tree. Apple sync source is identical at `HEAD` and tag `v3.9.0`; backend production is `196fe8d`. Findings N22 to N27 below; packages CS1, SE1, SM1 and TG1 in the plan.

Limits. The Mac's local diagnostics file could not be read (macOS privacy), so client behaviour comes from source plus server logs. N24's cause is traced in code and matches the logs, including the imported-meeting exception, but no test reproduces it yet; SM1.1 does that before any fix. The three-device case and both wrong-side cases in N24 are derived from the code, not observed. The recording sample covers 28 hours. Vercel log reads return at most 50 distinct rows per call, so counts taken from logs are lower bounds.

### Decisions and detector experiment, later on 2026-11-14

Ian answered the three decisions the same day. CS-1: keep the cloud switch fix in the plan; it is a backend change only, and the deploy still needs the per-device check and his go-ahead. TG-1: stop emailing for a transcript gap on its own and alert only on real problems, with the definition left to the plan. TG-2: he asked for it to be figured out by investigation.

That investigation made one provider call beyond the reads above: about 14 minutes of audio streamed to Deepgram with the backend's own key, which was loaded from the local environment file and never printed, at a cost of roughly 10 US cents. The audio was a BBC World Service capture and generated silence, noise and tones. Nothing of any customer was sent. The script, method and numbers are kept in `scripts/stall-probe/` in the Apple repository. It also read segment times and text lengths, not text, of two of Ian's own meetings, and the existing recovery events for the 28-hour sample.

Results. A healthy socket sent a message at least every 1.13 seconds on silence, noise, tones and speech. `SpeechStarted` appeared 0 times in 3 minutes of non-speech and 15 times in 60 seconds of speech, 0.08 seconds after speech began; the first words took 0.5 to 1.2 seconds. Replaying the app's forced reconnect at five points in continuous speech lost 7, 4, 12, 13 and 12 words against a reference that repeated exactly (168 of 168). In the field sample, words returned within 2 seconds of the new connection after 15 of 107 forced reconnects and within 5 seconds after 46, so reconnects do land on speech. Ian's alerted call of 13 Nov has words only from 5:40 to 36:14 of 41 minutes, with every detection outside that span.

Conclusion for TG-2: change the detector (TG1.4). A dead socket and plain noise can both be told apart directly, and the loudness rule costs about a sentence when it fires during speech. Limits: one speech source, one network, one day; whether a live socket can fail to hear real speech is not tested, which is why TG1.4 keeps a slow fallback and TG1.3 checks real recordings before release.
